## 1 Name of the medicinal product
Corvanex 1 mg solution for injection in pre-filled pen. The active substance is corvatide, a long-acting incretin receptor agonist.

## 2 Qualitative and quantitative composition
Each pre-filled pen delivers 1 mg corvatide in 0.5 mL solution. Excipients: sodium phosphate, propylene glycol, phenol and water for injections. The product contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per dose and is essentially sodium-free.

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications
Corvanex is indicated for the treatment of adults aged 18 years and over with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus, as an adjunct to diet and exercise, either alone or in combination with other glucose-lowering medicines. Corvanex is not indicated for type 1 diabetes, for weight management or for any other condition.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration
The recommended dose is 1 mg corvatide injected subcutaneously once weekly, on the same day each week, at any time of day, with or without meals. Corvanex is injected into the abdomen, thigh or upper arm, and the injection site should be rotated each week. If a dose is missed, it should be given as soon as possible within 3 days (72 hours) of the missed dose; if more than 3 days have passed, the missed dose should be skipped and the next dose given on the regularly scheduled day. No dose adjustment is required in patients aged 65 years and over. No dose adjustment is required in mild or moderate renal impairment; Corvanex is not recommended in severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease. The safety and efficacy of Corvanex in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established.

## 4.3 Contraindications
Hypersensitivity to corvatide or to any of the excipients. Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma. Multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2.

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use
Corvanex must not be used in patients with type 1 diabetes or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis. Acute pancreatitis has been observed; patients should be informed of the characteristic symptoms of acute pancreatitis (persistent, severe abdominal pain) and Corvanex should be discontinued if pancreatitis is suspected. Gastrointestinal side effects may cause dehydration, which can lead to a deterioration of renal function. Patients treated with Corvanex in combination with a sulfonylurea or insulin may have an increased risk of hypoglycaemia.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction
Corvanex delays gastric emptying and may affect the absorption of orally administered medicines; no clinically relevant effect was seen with paracetamol, metformin, atorvastatin or oral contraceptives. When Corvanex is added to insulin or a sulfonylurea, a reduction in the dose of insulin or sulfonylurea should be considered to reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia. In patients taking warfarin, more frequent monitoring of INR is recommended when Corvanex is started.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation
There are insufficient data on the use of Corvanex in pregnant women. Corvanex should not be used during pregnancy and should be discontinued at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy. It is not known whether corvatide is excreted in human breast milk; there are insufficient data. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, and women planning a pregnancy, should consult their healthcare professional.

## 4.8 Undesirable effects
Very common side effects (may affect more than 1 in 10 people): nausea and diarrhoea. Common side effects (may affect up to 1 in 10 people): vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, decreased appetite, injection site reactions and hypoglycaemia when used with insulin or a sulfonylurea. Uncommon side effects (may affect up to 1 in 100 people): acute pancreatitis and gallstones. Suspected adverse reactions should be reported to the marketing authorisation holder's pharmacovigilance department.

## 4.9 Overdose
Overdose may cause severe nausea, vomiting and hypoglycaemia. Appropriate supportive treatment should be started according to the patient's clinical signs and symptoms. A prolonged period of observation may be necessary due to the long half-life of corvatide.

## 6.3 Shelf life
2 years. After first use or removal from the refrigerator, the pen can be stored for up to 14 days.

## 6.4 Special precautions for storage
Before first use, store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze, and do not use Corvanex if it has been frozen. After first use or removal from the refrigerator, the pen can be stored below 25°C for up to 14 days. Keep the pen cap on to protect from light.
